Cruelty in Immigration Enforcement

During Donald Trump’s presidential campaign, he often talked about going after the “worst of the worst” in immigration enforcement. That was never going to be the case, because illegal immigrants simply do not commit violent crime at the rate that Trump has claimed. We are now seeing that reflected in the data, with the vast majority of deportees or detainees having little-to-no criminal record.

In early October, ICE agents raided a Chicago apartment building in the middle of the night, with agents breaking down doors and a helicopter hovering overhead. The justification for the raid was that the building was “filled with TdA terrorists,” according to White House homeland security advisor Stephen Miller. A Pro Publica investigation identified 21 of the 37 immigrants detained in the raid (federal officials declined to release the list). The investigation found that no criminal charges have been filed against anyone in the raid, and no criminal convictions at all for 18 of the 21 individuals. Federal officials have not provided evidence of Tren de Aragua membership for the two individuals they claim were in the gang. To sum up, families were detained in the middle of the night, had children taken detained in their pajamas, and had their apartments ransacked, all so the Trump administration could press zero charges. But at least they got a neat video of agents repelling from helicopters and breaking doors down.

On a broader scale, CBS News looked into over 600 individuals detained by ICE in the Chicago area. Just 16 of them had criminal histories that merited “high risk” designations by the federal government.

Selective Enforcement

The Trump administration has upended the Department of Justice, choosing not to enforce laws against favored parties, while demanding investigations that result in any type of conviction against disfavored parties.

The New York Times interviewed 60+ DOJ officials who have been fired or resigned to get their stories. Some of the highlights include Trump administration officials:

  • Stopping enforcement of laws that prevent companies giving bribes in foreign countries and transparency requirements for people working for foreign countries.
  • Demanding charges be brought against universities, rather than allowing an investigation to determine whether charges are justified.
  • Ordering FBI field offices to spend at least 1/3 of their time on immigration enforcement, removing resources from ongoing investigations of violent crime, drug trafficking, and child exploitation.
  • Demanding charges be brought against James Comey, and firing or forcing resignation from prosecutors who said that there was not sufficient evidence. (The Comey case has now hit a rough patch because the prosecutor put in place of those fired/resigned officials is now being cited for prosecutorial misconduct.)

An important quote from one of the officials in the story: “I really fear the Justice Department — which has a good record of convicting dangerous, violent people — won’t see the same success rate, because people will think it’s not on the level.”

The focus on illegal immigration has also taken away law enforcement focus from other priorities. According to government documents, Department of Homeland Security officials spent 33% fewer hours on child exploitation cases from February through April compared to previous years. Other teams that have been diverted to illegal immigration assignments include those working on tracking black market oil from Iran, antiterrorism intelligence, training of federal law enforcement (including the Secret Service), and human trafficking. Corruption

US tariffs on Switzerland were reduced from 39% to 15% this week. There isn’t anything wrong with reducing tariffs, typically. However, in this case, tariffs were only reduced after a Swiss delegation visited the White House and gifted President Trump an engraved gold bar and a Rolex desk clock (Source). It is yet another example of the transactional nature of this presidency–that President Trump will often only provides benefits for a country or company if he personally has gotten something out of the deal.

The Department of Homeland Security has spent more than $200 million on an ad campaign intended to discourage illegal immigration. ProPublica found that the largest recipient of those contracts is one with significant ties to Department of Homeland Security officials. The subcontractor’s CEO is the husband of the DHS chief spokesperson, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem has previously used the Ohio-based firm for her own gubernatorial campaigns and other South Dakota projects, and Noem adviser Corey Lewandowski has previously worked with the firm. DHS has claimed that they had no involvement in the selection. However, the declared “national emergency” at the border meant that normal competitive bidding processes were bypassed, so there is little transparency in the process short of a Congressional inquiry.
